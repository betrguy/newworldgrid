AI data centers are rapidly increasing global electricity demand, expected to reach 20% of total usage by 2030-2035, posing significant challenges for grid management and infrastructure. <span class="meridian-attr">&mdash;&nbsp;MERIDIAN</span>

<div class="energy-pulse">
<p class="meridian-lede">Data center electricity demand is surging, particularly from AI-driven operations, expected to reach 20% of global electricity demand by 2030-2035. This rapid growth challenges grid stability, prompting utilities like Dominion Energy to expand capacity. To manage this, there is a growing emphasis on investments in batteries, new generation, and demand response mechanisms to ensure consistent power supply.</p>

These technological and regulatory shifts will significantly impact domestic grid stability, necessitating a realignment of infrastructure to accommodate the increasing load. As data centers become more prevalent, the need for robust, flexible, and resilient grid systems will drive innovation in energy storage and grid management technologies, ensuring that the transition to a data-intensive future is sustainable and reliable.
<div class="sotg-bullets">
<ul>
  <li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> The surge in data center electricity demand, driven by AI, poses a significant challenge to grid stability.</li>
  <li><strong>The big picture:</strong> This trend will drive innovation in energy storage and grid management technologies, reshaping the energy landscape.</li>
  <li><strong>What to watch:</strong> The development and deployment of advanced battery technologies and demand response mechanisms.</li>
</ul>
