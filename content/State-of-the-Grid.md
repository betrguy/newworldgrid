The U.S. grid faces a significant challenge as market design rather than technology stands as the primary barrier to the widespread adoption of clean energy sources. <span class="meridian-attr">&mdash;&nbsp;MERIDIAN</span>

<div class="energy-pulse">
<p class="meridian-lede">The U.S. grid is experiencing a clean energy bottleneck due to market design issues, despite a record 17% share of electricity generated by wind and solar. This highlights the need for more flexible and adaptive market structures to support the integration of renewable energy sources.</p>

Geopolitical tensions, exemplified by the United States' sanctions on Russia’s A7 payment system, underscore the interdependence of financial and energy systems. These actions could disrupt global energy markets and supply chains, impacting grid stability and operational costs.
<div class="sotg-bullets">
<ul>
  <li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> The U.S. grid's clean energy bottleneck and geopolitical tensions highlight vulnerabilities in energy market structures.</li>
  <li><strong>The big picture:</strong> These issues underscore the need for more resilient and interconnected energy systems to withstand geopolitical disruptions.</li>
  <li><strong>What to watch:</strong> The development of adaptive market designs that can handle increased renewable energy integration while maintaining grid stability.</li>
</ul>
