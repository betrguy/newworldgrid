<div class="kg-index-description">

The **Kilo Grid Index** (kG) is a momentum-weighted composite scored 0.0–5.0 that measures the acceleration of civilization-level physical infrastructure. It is derived from five components, each normalized against a ceiling and clamped to 1.0, then multiplied by its weight and summed: nuclear build-out momentum (30% — reactor, SMR, and fission news velocity in the 48-hour RSS window, ceiling 20 articles), compute build-out momentum (25% — data center and AI infrastructure news velocity, ceiling 15 articles), grid capacity delta (20% — ERCOT interconnection queue change vs. prior reading, ceiling +0.5 TW), electricity demand growth (15% — EIA year-over-year U.S. load growth %, ceiling 3%), and sovereign capital flow (10% — DoD hardware contract obligations over 30 days, ceiling $15B). The weighted sum is scaled to the 0–5 kG range. A score above 3.0 kG reflects coordinated acceleration across energy, compute, and industrial capital — the physical substrate of advanced civilization scaling in real time.
