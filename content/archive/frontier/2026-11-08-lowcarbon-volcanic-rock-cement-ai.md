### **The High Ground**

US intelligence warns that China is enhancing its capability to disrupt satellites in orbit, highlighting the need for other countries to be more cautious about their space assets. This development underscores the importance of space stations, such as the one China recently expanded, for training and maintenance. Meanwhile, Russia is preoccupied with emergencies in southern Ukraine, which may temporarily divert their attention from space-related activities. As a result, the orbital economy is becoming more complex, and international cooperation is essential to ensure the smooth operation of space assets.

### **The EV Race**

Ford unveiled a 30% more efficient EV platform, challenging BYD in the market, according to a report from Notebookcheck on November 8, 2026. This advancement could shift buyer preferences towards Ford vehicles, offering better range and performance. Manufacturers will need to innovate to keep up, while grid operators may face increased demand for charging infrastructure to support the growing EV fleet.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Imagine it’s 2036, and the orbital economy has matured, with constellations of satellites orbiting Earth in perfect harmony, serving everything from navigation to communication. One day, a small, agile satellite named *EchoStar-28* is launched by a Chinese company, *New Horizon Space Technologies*. *EchoStar-28* is not just another communication satellite; it’s equipped with a cutting-edge quantum entanglement system. This system creates a network of satellites that can instantly communicate with each other, even when separated by vast distances, effectively creating a quantum internet in space.

This technology has a strange second-order effect: It disrupts the traditional business model of satellite-based internet providers like *SpaceX’s Starlink*. Suddenly, the need for vast numbers of satellites to maintain global internet coverage diminishes. The quantum internet in space can theoretically provide faster, more secure connections with fewer satellites. This leads to a significant reduction in the demand for traditional satellite launches, affecting not just SpaceX but also other launch providers like *ArianeGroup* and *United Launch Alliance*.

The impact ripples through the space economy, forcing a reevaluation of launch vehicle and satellite design. Companies that focus on traditional hardware may struggle, while those investing in quantum technologies and new forms of space-based communication stand to gain. Nations, like the United States and China, may find their strategic advantages shifting as quantum capabilities redefine the landscape of space-based communications.
