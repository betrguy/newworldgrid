### **The High Ground**

GAC's new E8 EV in Macao marks a step forward in electric vehicle technology, as reported by The EV Report. However, the real shift is in space. President Trump's pledge to protect Elon Musk, as reported by Axios, suggests potential regulatory changes that could boost private space efforts. With the U.S. military eyeing OrbisTech's modular OrbisHubs for space weapons, the orbital economy is becoming a battlefield. Companies like OrbisTech are now key players, offering flexible space stations that can serve both civilian and military needs. This dual-use approach could reshape the space industry, as commercial and military interests converge.

### **The EV Race**

Blink Charging has rebranded to Blink Energy, signaling its expansion into energy management systems (EMS) and battery technologies. This move indicates increased competition in the EV infrastructure market, as other providers may need to diversify their offerings to remain competitive. Grid operators should anticipate more complex demand patterns due to the growing presence of EMS and advanced battery solutions.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Five years from now, in 2031, the space economy has taken a surprising turn. In an unexpected twist, GAC's new E8 electric vehicle, launched in Macao with a 175-mile range, has found a niche in the orbital economy. Blink Energy, the rebranded version of Blink Charging, has begun integrating EV charging stations in geostationary orbit, using its EMS (Energy Management System) and battery technology. This setup has created a novel scenario: EVs in orbit are now charged by solar panels on these stations, with the energy then beamed down to Earth via microwave transmission. This has led to a significant reduction in launch costs for small satellites, as they can now piggyback on the energy grid, effectively turning the space above Earth into a giant recharging station. The strategic implications are profound; nations and private companies are now vying for control over these orbital charging hubs, turning the old space race into a battle for energy dominance.
