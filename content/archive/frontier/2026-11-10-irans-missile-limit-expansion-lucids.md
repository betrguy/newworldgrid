### **The High Ground**

Russia is expanding its orbital infrastructure through the CSTO, adding more satellites for space-based energy and compute. According to the evidence, this move strengthens Russia's presence in the orbital industry, making it harder for other nations to operate in the region. Additionally, Iran has lifted the 2,000km limit on its ballistic missiles, potentially enabling more ambitious space launches and deployments. These developments signal a growing emphasis on space-based capabilities, driving the expansion of orbital infrastructure.

### **The EV Race**

Lucid CEO’s statement that they “didn’t even know where cars were” at the start of Q3 highlights the growing complexity of the electric vehicle market. This admission underscores the need for advanced tracking and management systems to optimize supply chains and ensure efficient delivery of EVs. Manufacturers must invest in robust logistics to meet increasing demand. Grid operators are also facing challenges in managing the surge in EV charging, emphasizing the need for smarter, more integrated energy solutions.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Imagine it’s 2036, and a small satellite constellation launched by private firm Orbital Dynamics is suddenly inoperable. The constellation, designed to provide high-resolution imaging for global security and environmental monitoring, was crucial for tracking illegal fishing activities and drug trafficking. Initially, the failure was attributed to a single, undetected manufacturing flaw. However, further investigation reveals that the satellite’s failure was a result of a rare solar flare that exposed a previously unknown vulnerability in the constellation’s software. This flaw, discovered after months of trial-and-error, turns out to be a backdoor inserted by a nation-state, likely Russia, as part of its CSTO alliance efforts. The backdoor, undetectable in normal operations, allowed for the precise timing of the failure. This incident sparks a global debate on the reliability of space assets and the need for stricter cybersecurity measures in space infrastructure. The revelation also forces companies like Orbital Dynamics and their competitors to re-evaluate their supply chains, potentially leading to a shift in the balance of power in the orbital economy.
