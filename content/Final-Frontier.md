## Frontier Brief — Mar 24, 2026

**The High Ground**

Today's significant signal comes from SpaceX's Mission: Moon - Human Landing System (HLS). For the first time in over 50 years, Starship will land humans on the lunar surface under NASA. This marks a major milestone in the development of Starship-class vehicles, which are poised to revolutionize the logistics industry by collapsing the cost of moving mass to space. With Starship HLS, SpaceX is demonstrating its ability to transport not only cargo but also crew to the Moon and back, paving the way for the establishment of lunar industrial parks.

The implications of this development are far-reaching. As Starship becomes more capable, it will enable the creation of self-sustaining lunar bases that can support heavy industry, such as solar panel manufacturing and in-orbit assembly. This, in turn, will drive down the cost of space-based energy production and reduce reliance on Earth-based supplies.

The strategic balance has shifted significantly with this development, as SpaceX is now poised to become a major player in the lunar logistics market. The company's ability to transfer humans and cargo to the Moon will open up new opportunities for industry and commerce, marking the beginning of a new era for space-based resource utilization.

**The Terrestrial Engine**

In the terrestrial engine, two significant developments caught our attention: BYD's warning as electric vehicles face new tax in Australia, and CATL and BYD driving China's next energy frontier with sodium-ion and perovskite solar. The former highlights the increasing complexity of EV taxation, which is expected to have a significant impact on consumer demand and industry growth.

In terms of technology foundation, China remains the leader in battery production, with CATL and BYD continuing to drive innovation in sodium-ion and perovskite solar cells. In contrast, the US lags behind in these areas, with companies like Tesla struggling to compete with Chinese manufacturers on cost and efficiency.

The implications of this technological gap are significant, as China's lead in battery production and solar energy could give it a strategic advantage in the transition to an electric-based economy.

**Frontier Speculation: The 0.1% Shift**

What follows is purely a thought experiment. Today's news about SpaceX's Mission: Moon and the emergence of Orbital Data Centers as a new frontier raises an intriguing question: what if wireless power transmission becomes feasible for long-range applications? If this were to happen, it could revolutionize space-based computing by eliminating the need for terrestrial data transfer.

With wireless power transmission, orbital compute nodes could be designed with much smaller antennae and receivers, reducing their mass and increasing their efficiency. This would enable the creation of more compact, high-performance AI systems that can operate in space without relying on expensive terrestrial connectivity. The implications of such a breakthrough would be profound, as it could enable the development of decentralized, AI-powered space-based infrastructure that supports global industries and economies.

However, this is purely speculative at this point, and significant technical hurdles need to be overcome before wireless power transmission becomes feasible for long-range applications.
