### **The High Ground**

Orbital infrastructure continues to be a critical asset. Today, Pakistan and China launched an information corridor, setting up 18 outlets for news exchange and fact-checking. This enhances communication and data reliability in space, supporting robust orbital compute and resource extraction operations. Additionally, the Palestinian question remains a core issue in the Middle East, as highlighted by the UN chief.

### **The EV Race**

BYD has opened reservations for its Second-Gen Seal 07 model with flash charging, as reported by The EV Report. This new feature allows for quick charging, reducing wait times at stations. For buyers, faster charging means more convenience.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

In 2036, the orbital economy is bustling with activity. A small company, QuantumTech, has been quietly developing a new type of satellite called the "SkyHarvester." These satellites are designed not for communication or imaging, but to harvest space debris and convert it into raw materials for 3D printing in orbit. The process is efficient and sustainable, but the real shock comes when it's discovered that the SkyHarvester's byproduct includes rare isotopes critical for advanced quantum computing. Suddenly, QuantumTech isn't just a debris cleanup firm; it's become a key player in the global tech market. Nations and corporations scramble to secure contracts, leading to a new era of strategic dependency and economic warfare in space.
