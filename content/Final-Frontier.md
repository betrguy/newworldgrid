<!-- FRONTIER_ENGINE_START -->

### **The High Ground**

Nio is navigating a brutal price war in the EV market, but investors might be missing out. Meanwhile, President Trump issues an ultimatum to Iran, but the orbital economy continues to grow. Companies like Orbital Energy and Compute are expanding their missions to extract resources from asteroids and the moon, setting up orbital data centers and factories. These space-based operations need reliable power, and advancements in satellite-to-satellite power beaming could transform how these industries operate. As more satellites are launched, the need for robust cybersecurity increases, driving a new focus on space-based security.

### **The EV Race**

Tesla surpassed Q3 delivery expectations, according to CnEVPost, but continues to trail BYD in BEV sales. This week, buyers in the EV market saw a mix of good and bad news. On one hand, Tesla met its delivery targets, which is a positive sign for its manufacturing and supply chain. On the other hand, the gap with BYD widens, highlighting BYD’s strong market position. For manufacturers, this disparity suggests a need to innovate and possibly explore new markets to maintain competitiveness.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Imagine a future where the space economy has shifted dramatically. In 2036, the once-staid space logistics firm, Orbital Kinetics, unveils a revolutionary new propulsion system. This system, called the "Quantum Thruster," is based on a theoretical breakthrough in quantum entanglement. Despite initial skepticism, the thruster proves to be vastly more efficient and reliable than any previous technology.

However, the true impact comes when Orbital Kinetics partners with a small but influential nation, Qatar, to deploy these thrusters on a fleet of communication satellites. The partnership allows Qatar to gain unprecedented control over global communications, enabling it to enforce data sovereignty and censorship across multiple continents. This move not only disrupts the existing satellite market but also forces a reevaluation of international data privacy laws and national security policies.
