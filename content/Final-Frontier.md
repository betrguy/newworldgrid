## Frontier Brief — Mar 27, 2026

**The High Ground**

SpaceX's recent filing with the FCC proposes launching up to 1 million LEO satellites for an orbital data center constellation, dwarfing every previous proposal. This development shifts the strategic balance by establishing a robust and scalable infrastructure for low-latency computing in orbit. With this capability, SpaceX can focus on deploying orbital data centers (ODCs) that will enable industries like finance, healthcare, and transportation to access high-performance computing from space.

The implications of this launch cadence are significant, with the potential to reduce costs compared to traditional approaches. This development also underscores the importance of LEO in supporting the growth of the orbital economy. SpaceX's Starship HLS launch has already marked a significant milestone for lunar landing capabilities, and this proposal takes it a step further by leveraging the same technology to support ODCs.

The cost implications of this development are substantial, with estimates suggesting that the constellation could be launched at an unprecedented scale. This proposal highlights the potential for SpaceX's Starship to play a critical role in enabling the deployment of ODCs and supporting the growth of the orbital economy.

**The Terrestrial Engine**

China's BYD has seen its first profit drop since 2021, despite still maintaining its position as a leading electric vehicle (EV) manufacturer. The US remains ahead of China in terms of EV technology and manufacturing capabilities, with companies like General Motors and Ford investing heavily in EV production. The challenges facing the global EV supply chain, including issues related to battery production and sourcing, are becoming increasingly apparent.

The profit drop at BYD underscores the intense competition in the EV market, with Tesla vying for dominance alongside BYD. The US is leading the charge in terms of EV technology development, while China is focusing on expanding its market share through aggressive pricing and marketing strategies. This competition will likely drive innovation and lower costs for consumers.

BYD's profit drop also highlights the need for industry-wide collaboration to address supply chain challenges. As the global EV market continues to grow, it will be essential to develop more efficient and sustainable production methods to meet increasing demand.

**Frontier Speculation: The 0.1% Shift**

*What follows is purely a thought experiment. This is a forward-looking scenario by ARC.*

If space-based solar power (SBSP) becomes commercially viable, enabled by advancements in orbital demos from 2026-2030, it could have a profound impact on the global energy landscape. With SBSP, the need for traditional fossil fuel-based infrastructure could be significantly reduced, potentially leading to a 20-30% drop in electricity costs due to reduced transmission losses.

As the first gigawatt-scale deployment of SBSP approaches, this technology could enable a strategic pivot towards decoupling energy generation from terrestrial sources. By harnessing energy from space and beaming it back to Earth, industries like transportation and manufacturing could become increasingly dependent on clean energy sources, driving innovation and reducing costs.

This scenario highlights the potential for SBSP to disrupt traditional energy models and drive a global transition to clean energy. As the technology continues to advance, we can expect to see significant changes in the way we generate, distribute, and consume energy.
