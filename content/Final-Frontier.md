### **The High Ground**

China’s growing fleet of intelligence satellites, as reported to aid in targeting Taiwan, indicates an expanding orbital infrastructure. This development underscores the strategic importance of satellite networks. Meanwhile, the integration of solar panels into Russia’s Rassvet network by Qihang Space suggests advancements in space-based solar power, which could transform the energy landscape.

Green Rain Energy has expanded its EV charging network nationwide, as reported in "Electric Vehicles Today - Green Rain Energy Expands EV Charging Network Nationwide" on Yahoo Finance (2026-10-30). This expansion provides more charging stations across the country, making it easier for drivers to switch to electric vehicles. For manufacturers, this expansion opens up new markets and increases competition. Grid operators will need to manage higher electricity demand during peak charging times.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Imagine a future where China’s burgeoning fleet of intelligence satellites, part of its broader military modernization, unexpectedly leads to a global privacy crisis. By 2036, the advanced capabilities of these satellites enable real-time surveillance over vast territories, including critical infrastructure and private communications. To counter this, a coalition of nations, including the United States and European Union, form an alliance to establish a new, highly secure, and encrypted satellite network. This initiative, led by a consortium including SpaceX and Telesat, aims to create a robust, privacy-respecting alternative to China’s surveillance capabilities. However, the unexpected consequence is a boom in the market for quantum encryption technology. Companies like QuantumSecure and Qubitekk, which had been developing quantum communication solutions, see a surge in demand. This shift not only reshapes the space economy but also fundamentally alters the global telecommunications landscape, ensuring a level of data security that was previously unattainable.
