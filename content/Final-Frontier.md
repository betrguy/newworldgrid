### **The High Ground**

Starlink’s missile tracker has reportedly been shut down, according to CBS News, as Russia launched new air strikes. This disruption could impact Ukraine’s ability to track incoming missiles, highlighting the critical role of space-based systems in modern warfare. Additionally, Musk stated that any limits on Ukraine's Starlink access are set by the U.S., not SpaceX itself. Ukraine reports that Starlink access has improved but is still facing speed limits, which are disrupting its battlefield operations.

### **The EV Race**

A new development in EV infrastructure has been reported. According to [ev market] news, the Arizona Department of Transportation (ADOT) has completed the installation of its first EV charging stations, but none are planned for Graham and Greenlee counties. This information comes from GilaValleyCentral.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Imagine it's 2036, and the world's focus has shifted to the high ground of space. In this future, SpaceX's Starlink constellation has become a critical backbone for global communications, with over 50,000 satellites orbiting Earth. However, a strange second-order effect emerges: the nation-state of Ukraine, once a mere user of Starlink services, has quietly begun to repurpose some of these satellites. Specifically, they've integrated a handful of Starlink satellites into a new, covert constellation designed for precision tracking of Russian missile launches. This unexpected shift, facilitated by the shutdown of Starlink trackers as reported, has created a de facto space-based early warning system. The U.S. government, recognizing this capability, decides to provide limited access to the constellation to NATO allies, effectively turning the tables on Russian missile defense. This move not only changes the dynamics of the ongoing conflict but also sets a precedent for the militarization of commercial space assets, potentially reshaping the global space economy and international relations in ways no one foresaw.
