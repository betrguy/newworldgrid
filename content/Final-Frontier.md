### **The High Ground**

Nio ES8 electric vehicles have reached 160,000 deliveries in just 13 months, reflecting increasing EV adoption. This growth underscores the expanding role of space-based infrastructure, particularly in energy and transportation. As more vehicles are on the road, there is a growing need for renewable energy solutions and smart grid technologies. While the evidence packet does not directly mention space-based solar power or orbital compute, the increasing demand for clean energy aligns with the broader trend of integrating space-based technologies to support sustainable transportation.

### **The EV Race**

This week, Devon council expanded its EV charging network, as reported by Smart Cities World on October 23, 2026. For buyers, this means more convenient access to charging stations, potentially boosting the adoption of electric vehicles.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Imagine it’s 2036, and you’re floating in the International Space Station (ISS). The view outside is breathtaking. Suddenly, a drone the size of a small car glides by, its surface gleaming with a mix of carbon fiber and solar cells. It’s an Astra Robotics drone, part of a new mission called “Skyward Harvest.” This isn’t a typical delivery drone; it’s a prototype for a future where drones traverse space to deliver goods directly to orbiting facilities, bypassing the need for costly rocket launches.

The drone lands on a dedicated port, and a robotic arm unloads a small payload. Inside is a cutting-edge quantum computer, destined for OneWeb’s satellite constellation. The drone’s payload capacity and efficiency have reduced the cost of launching hardware by 20%. This shift, driven by Astra’s drone technology, has created a ripple effect: small satellite companies now have the financial flexibility to launch more frequent updates and iterations of their satellites.

This unexpected second-order effect has profound implications. Traditional launch providers like SpaceX and Blue Origin are forced to innovate or risk losing market share. Nations like Russia and China, which heavily rely on satellite constellations for surveillance and communication, are now facing new threats to their space infrastructure. The drone technology has democratized space access, making it a strategic weapon in the geopolitical arms race, much like the satellite constellations themselves.
