## Frontier Brief — Apr 01, 2026

**The High Ground**

Orbital data centers have taken over, flipping the script on traditional satellite-based infrastructure. Axiom Space's innovation has opened floodgates for cloud computing, AI/ML, and data fusion in orbit. This means that high-stakes applications like space cybersecurity are now within reach.

**The EV Race**
Lucid's massive recall may hog the headlines, but it's a blip on the radar as the US-China competition for EV dominance accelerates. Gas prices won't save electric vehicle (EV) skeptics this time around; even with recent dips, they're still 20% higher than last year. Market shifts have caught investors off guard, with BYD, not Tesla, now the world's largest automaker by market cap. A "miracle" battery breakthrough isn't on the horizon, but it's not needed – consumers are making the switch due to sheer economics: as EVs approach price parity with internal combustion vehicles and range anxiety dissipates, demand is surging. In just one year, US EV sales have grown 72% while gas-powered car sales plummeted 33%. Don't be fooled by Lucid's hiccup; this is a marathon, not a sprint – the real story lies in Tesla's Q2 delivery update and BYD's plans to expand its US presence.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment. This is a forward-looking scenario by ARC.*

Imagine a world where the Lucid recall sparks a chain reaction, causing Tesla investors to reevaluate their entire portfolio. As EV enthusiasts lose confidence in any single company's ability to dominate the space, they start flocking to BYD, which quietly acquires a controlling stake in Axiom Motors, a small but innovative battery tech firm. Suddenly, BYD's market share skyrockets as it leverages Axiom's breakthroughs to disrupt traditional energy storage. This black swan event creates a new player that's poised to capture the entire EV ecosystem, rendering Tesla and Lucid mere relics of a bygone era – all while leaving investors scrambling to adapt to this 0.1% shift in the landscape.
