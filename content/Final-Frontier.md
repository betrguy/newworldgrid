The U.S. orbital economy continues to show resilience with companies like Rocket Lab and SpaceX performing well, while U.S. EV sales are expected to decline significantly, putting pressure on traditional automakers as Tesla, Toyota, and GM compete for market share. <span class="meridian-attr">&mdash;&nbsp;ARC</span>

<!-- FRONTIER_ENGINE_START -->

### **The High Ground**

Rocket Lab’s latest Electron launch has propelled the company’s stock 5% higher. Meanwhile, SpaceX, which received backing from Cathie Wood, has risen 3%, indicating strong investor confidence. AST SpaceMobile has gained 2% despite a price target cut to $30. The electric propulsion satellite market is expected to reach US$ 148.78, reflecting growth in the sector.

### **The EV Race**

Tesla, Toyota, and General Motors are competing for a 53% share of the U.S. electric vehicle market in 2026. According to CarbonCredits.com, Tesla's sales fell by 47% in 2026. The Ford Bronco EV is priced at $32,320 in China but has no U.S. launch date, suggesting a focus on international markets. BYD, China’s leading EV manufacturer, recently launched a Rolls-Royce competitor in China, showcasing its ability to innovate and compete with luxury brands.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Imagine it’s 2036. AST SpaceMobile, once a fledgling player in the space communication market, has quietly revolutionized satellite operations. Their fleet of nano-satellites, initially designed for low-cost, high-frequency data transmission, has now become a critical infrastructure for global financial networks. In a surprising twist, these satellites are equipped with a proprietary quantum encryption protocol, which has made them indispensable for secure transactions. This technology, while not directly related to their original mission, has transformed AST SpaceMobile into a key player in international trade and banking. The sudden demand has strained the company’s supply chain, leading to shortages of critical components. This scarcity, in turn, has forced satellite manufacturers like SpaceX and Rocket Lab to adjust their production lines to include these components, shifting the dynamics of the entire space launch industry. The ripple effect extends to insurance companies, who now face the challenge of pricing policies for launches that could potentially carry millions of dollars in sensitive financial data.
