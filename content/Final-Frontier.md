## Frontier Brief — Apr 04, 2026

**New Space Frontier**

A seismic shift is underway, as data centers are about to abandon traditional ground-based infrastructure for a revolutionary orbit-based approach. SpaceX's pioneering move to launch massive amounts of power infrastructure into Low Earth Orbit (LEO) is set to disrupt the entire cloud computing industry. Imagine computing capabilities at unprecedented speeds and with unparalleled reliability – all while circumventing connectivity issues on the ground.

The orbital data center revolution is about to rewrite the rules of high-performance computing, and SpaceX is leading the charge. By harnessing the vastness of space, we're entering an era where latency and connectivity become a thing of the past, enabling edge-computing applications that were previously unimaginable. This is not just a minor tweak; it's a full-on paradigm shift.

**The EV Race**

The US-China rivalry is intensifying as Tesla overtakes BYD in electric vehicle sales, fueled by a surge in interest despite having thousands of unsold units. Gas prices remain high, and market shifts indicate Americans are reevaluating their transportation options. Meanwhile, Chinese manufacturers' inability to penetrate the US market due to regulatory hurdles has forced them to focus on other regions. This dynamic is driving innovation, as seen in Tesla's move to expand its production capacity. With China accounting for a paltry 0.3% of US electric vehicle sales in 2025, it's no wonder they're targeting alternative markets.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment. This is a forward-looking scenario by ARC.*

Imagine a world where the unprecedented rise of electric vehicles, fueled by Tesla's market dominance and China's manufacturing prowess, creates a catastrophic shortage of rare earth elements essential for EV battery production. As Axiom Space's orbital data centers begin to launch in Q1 2027, the absence of these critical materials cripples the entire supply chain, causing a global panic that sends shockwaves through international relations. With Starship V3 poised to debut and SpaceX already having reached 30 missions in 2026, a desperate scramble ensues for alternative sources, as nations vie to secure access to previously inaccessible asteroid deposits rich in these essential resources. The result: a new frontier of space-based mining and the potential for a catastrophic trade war over control of the rare earth supply chain, all sparked by an unforeseen consequence of the electric vehicle revolution and the launch of orbital data centers.
