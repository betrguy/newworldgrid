### **The High Ground**

China is deploying robotic space weapons capable of attacking other satellites, as reported by Yahoo (2026-10-31). This development signals a new era in space-based combat and could disrupt the space economy, potentially prompting companies to enhance their defensive technologies or seek more robust providers. The orbital infrastructure is increasingly becoming a battlefield, with implications for various space-based industries.

### **The EV Race**

Zeekr 7X, a key rival to Tesla Model Y, is set to offer a 500kW flagship version with a range of up to 627km in 2027, at the same price point as the current model. This upgrade puts pressure on Tesla to innovate and compete, as manufacturers continue to push the boundaries of performance and efficiency. Grid operators should prepare for potential spikes in charging demand as more high-powered EVs enter the market.

*What follows is purely a thought experiment.*

Imagine it’s 2036, and a small, privately-owned firm called OrbisTech has quietly developed a new type of satellite—a modular, reconfigurable space station designed for scientific research and resource extraction. OrbisTech, originally known for its consumer drone tech, has pivoted into space infrastructure, aiming to revolutionize the orbital economy. These stations, dubbed OrbisHubs, can be launched as single units and then expanded as needed, making them cost-effective and adaptable.

The twist? The U.S. military, seeing the potential for strategic advantage, decides to use OrbisHubs not just for scientific research but also as launch platforms for their new fleet of robotic space weapons. These weapons are capable of deploying smaller, disposable satellites to disrupt enemy communications and surveillance networks.

This shift means that space has become a new battlefield, with commercial and military interests intertwining in ways never before seen. OrbisTech, initially a small player, has now become a key player in the space economy, its modular design and reconfigurability making it a preferred choice for both civilian and military applications. The strategic consequences are profound: the U.S. gains a critical edge in space surveillance and attack capabilities, while OrbisTech’s stock soars, transforming it from a niche player into a global tech giant.
