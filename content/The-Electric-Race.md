## Daily Electric Brief — Mar 18, 2026

**Today's Briefing**

Stellantis and Factorial Energy have reached a key milestone in solid-state battery development. The company is optimizing high energy density, cycle life, and fast charging capabilities. Western carmakers are adopting LFP cells at a slower rate than expected.

The Chinese EV grid remains dominated by CATL and BYD. In January 2026, CATL held a market share of 49.79% with a battery installation volume of 13.2GWh, followed by BYD at 17.43%. The Asia Pacific EV battery market is expected to reach $138.18 billion in 2035.

Rapid-charging ultra-fast charging systems are being developed to refill electric vehicle batteries quickly. Advancements in thermal adhesives are enhancing battery cell-to-pack and cell-to-ribbon performance for EVs. Cargo Matters to invest INR 66 crore in a smart EV charging network across South India.

---

**The American Front**

**Ford**
Ford's single most important strategic priority is to build a credible commercial and fleet charging network to offset its hemorrhaging losses on EV production. The company has not yet established a significant presence in the North American market, where it can leverage its F-150 Lightning truck-first install base.

**GM**
GM's primary focus should be on delivering a single, deliverable win with its Ultium battery platform and charging infrastructure. Despite its manufacturing scale and mass-market reach, GM has ceded software and charging expertise to other companies, exposing a structural gap in its ecosystem.

**Tesla**
Tesla's priority remains defending its charging network as the de facto U.S. standard while China builds its own global footprint. The company owns the Supercharger network, battery supply relationships, and software update cycle, giving it a significant advantage over Western competitors.

**Thought Exercise: Three Startups That Could Leap China**

* **Chargeway**: A startup that develops modular, urban-scale charging infrastructure for EVs, targeting cities with limited grid capacity. Chargeway's platform would enable efficient, high-power charging hubs for both public and private fleets.
* **Electriq**: A company that creates advanced battery recycling technologies to recover valuable materials from spent batteries. Electriq's focus on closed-loop recycling could significantly reduce the environmental impact of EV manufacturing and provide a new revenue stream for manufacturers.
* **Mineralux**: A startup that develops AI-powered mineral prospecting and sourcing platforms to optimize lithium, cobalt, and other critical battery materials. Mineralux would help Western companies overcome their supply chain weaknesses by providing real-time, data-driven insights into material availability and prices.
